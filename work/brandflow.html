§ What it does

Brand in. Merch out. No middle.

Brandflow is a print-on-demand SaaS for UK indie brands. A merchant uploads their artwork, designs real garments in the browser, and connects their Shopify or WooCommerce store with one click.

Every order that lands then flows through to Inspiration's UK fulfilment centre — printed, packed and dispatched in 48 hours. No minimums, no setup fees, no stock to sit on. A logo at 10am can be a live product by lunch.

01 — Design

On the real garment.

Custom in-browser designer with two modes. Drop a logo on the shirt, switch colourway, hit save — composite mockups generate for every variant.

02 — Publish

Straight to your store.

One-click sync with Shopify or WooCommerce. Listing, variants, photography, pricing — generated and pushed live in seconds.

03 — Fulfil

UK-printed, 48 hours.

Inspiration prints, packs and dispatches every order from a single UK facility. The merchant never touches stock, ink, or a Royal Mail van.

§ The name

Brand · Flow

UK indie brands shouldn't need a warehouse, a screen printer's lead time and a £400 setup quote just to drop a tee. The promise was simple: remove every hurdle between an idea and a product live on a store.

So the name said it back. Brand — what they already have. Flow — what we give them. Upload a logo at 10am, ship orders by Friday. No minimums, no friction, no stock to sit on.

The wordmark holds the same idea visually — a single, looping B that doesn't lift the pen.

§ 06 — The Designer

Two tools in one canvas.

The hardest UX call on the project: most merchants just have a PNG of their logo. Showing them a Photoshop-style interface causes them to bounce on the first session.

But the merchants who are designers want every slider, every blend mode, every layer. The answer was two modes living inside the same canvas — toggled in the header, with the same artwork between them.

Simple mode

Just put it on the shirt.

One garment, one print area, two buttons. Click Upload artwork and your logo is on the tee. Click Change colour to swap the colourway. That's the whole interface.

  • No panels · no chrome
  • White canvas · zero intimidation
  • Drop-in for non-designers
Designer mode

Or build it from scratch.

Three-panel pro tool — shape library on the left, Konva canvas in the middle, properties / layers / artwork on the right. Same toggle, same canvas, same artwork stays in place.

  • Shapes · text · gradients · shadows
  • In-browser background removal
  • Layers · undo · keyboard shortcuts
§ 07 — Store integrations

Plug it into a store you already have.

Brandflow doesn't ask merchants to switch storefronts. It connects to the two systems they already use — Shopify and WooCommerce — and pushes finished products live without leaving the dashboard.

Listings, variants, retail prices, every per-colourway photograph and the entire size matrix — all generated and synced. Webhooks then catch every incoming order in real time and route it straight to fulfilment.

Shopify

OAuth in, webhooks out.

A merchant clicks "Connect Shopify", approves the app on their store, and Brandflow has scoped API access. Publishing a product creates the Shopify listing, variants and per-colourway images in a single call. Orders are pushed back via webhook the second they're placed.

  • OAuth · scoped access
  • Product · variant · image API
  • Order webhook ingestion
WooCommerce

REST keys, same flow.

For WordPress shops, Brandflow takes a pair of WooCommerce REST keys and treats the store identically — the merchant never sees a different UI. Same designer, same publish button, same order pipeline. The integration layer abstracts away the platform differences so the studio runs on one workflow.

  • WooCommerce REST API
  • Product + variation sync
  • Order polling + webhook
§ 08 — Under the hood It only feels this simple because of this.
Where the design met the engineering
Each call removes a hurdle the merchant never sees
01 — Canvas

A canvas that knows where it can print.

The artwork layer is hard-clipped to the printable zone using Konva's clipFunc. Drag a logo off the shirt and it physically can't render there. Rectangular zones use bounding-box clipping; oddly-shaped items like keyrings use the actual SVG path. The merchant can't make a "ruined at print" mistake.

Konva.js · clipFunc · SVG path masks
02 — AI in the browser

Background removal, no server.

An ONNX neural network runs the whole background-removal model client-side in a Web Worker. Two seconds, no API key, no per-image fee, no upload — the merchant clicks once and the white box around their logo disappears. The model is ~30MB and lazy-loads only when the tool is opened.

@imgly/background-removal · ONNX · WASM
03 — Composite engine

Print-ready mockups in a heartbeat.

On save, an HTML canvas pipeline composites the artwork onto every selected colourway at 1260×1680 — three times the design canvas — so Shopify's image scaler never has to upscale. The output: a full set of retina-crisp product shots, one per variant, generated in under a second.

HTML canvas · per-variant export · Supabase upload
04 — Direct-to-cloud

Big files stop being a problem.

Vercel's serverless cap is 4.5MB per request — print-resolution artwork sails past that. Brandflow issues signed upload URLs so the browser pushes files straight into Supabase Storage, bypassing the server entirely. Upload speed scales with the merchant's connection, not the API.

Supabase Storage · signed URLs · streamed upload
